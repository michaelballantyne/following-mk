# Do we need stronger reasoning than unit propagation? (CDCL evaluation)

Michael's question (2026-07-12): given the information sources we have
(and are adding), is plain determinacy-directed propagation enough, or
would a stronger principle — CDCL-style conflict learning in particular —
pay? Analysis note; one new measurement (per-level ID breakdown, below),
the rest argued from numbers already in the record. Verdict at the end.

## What "unit propagation" currently means here

The follower is a unit-propagation engine over candidate structure: a
`conde/d` fires only when exactly one clause survives (unit), and the
views are theory propagators contributing refutations (clause deletion)
and occasionally forcings (unit implications). Three things it does NOT
have: **memory across branches** (a refutation teaches nothing beyond
killing its branch), **memory across ID levels** (each level re-derives
everything), and **lookahead at stalls** (a 2-live-clause conde/d
contributes nothing even when both clauses agree on consequences).
Those are three separable gaps; CDCL addresses the first two,
lookahead/SAC the third. Taking them in turn:

## Gap 1: cross-level re-refutation in ID — bounded at ~2.7×, and not a CDCL problem

Measured today on the converged rember config (tv4ex, reproduced
312,236 unify(main) exactly): per-level unify(main) is

    15:2,584  19:2,584  23:4,239  27:6,299  31:16,423
    35:22,808  39:35,218  43:108,025  47:114,056(answer)

Size-closed levels are cumulative (bound N re-explores everything under
bound N−1), so *perfect* cross-level retention — process each candidate
once, at the first level it fits — collapses the total toward the final
level alone: 114,056 vs 312,236 = **2.74× ceiling** on what any
cross-level memory can buy on rember. The geometric bound schedule
already caps this waste at a small constant factor, and the
explicit-search design (backlog Now: frontier priority queue over the
first-order rep) achieves the same 1× *by construction*, with no lemma
machinery — a retained frontier never re-enumerates. Conclusion: real
but small, and owned by the explicit-search item, not by CDCL.

## Gap 2: cross-branch shared-reason families — views ARE learned lemmas, learned by hand

The strongest pro-CDCL datum in the record is LOO-NV on rember: remove
the one-line vacuous-conditional view and EX re-refutes that family one
candidate at a time — 962M unify(follower), 2.4× unify(main), 13× wall.
That is precisely the cost of a missing *generalized* nogood, paid in
per-candidate re-derivation. Each rung of the view ladder is a lemma we
learned offline: sample the surviving stream, diagnose a doomed family,
write the generalization. CDCL automates that loop in-search.

Three facts temper the case:

1. **The loop converged.** After five views, two further principled
   attempts (4b, 4c) found zero residue on rember. Post-convergence
   there is no large shared-reason family left for a learner to find —
   on this suite, human lemma-learning saturated the opportunity.
2. **The lemmas transfer.** The same five-view stack, with zero
   task-specific additions, is the best config on all three tasks.
   Hand-learned lemmas amortize across tasks; in-search learning pays
   only if *new* tasks keep producing *task-specific* families, which we
   haven't yet observed (n=3, narrow suite — this is the real unknown).
3. **Position-generalization is the hard part, and views get it free.**
   mk's search tree already shares prefix-shared failure (a refuted
   prefix is never re-entered within a run), so a learned nogood only
   adds value when the same reason recurs at *different positions/
   contexts* — exactly what NV showed (`(if (= X X) …)` at any position).
   A raw conflict is position-specific; generalizing it into a
   position-generic pattern is the whole trick (Neo's "equivalence
   modulo conflict" does it through component specs). A hand-written
   view is born position-generic. So the honest comparison is not
   "CDCL vs nothing" but "automated generalization vs a human writing
   the ~40-line view in under an hour" — 4a through 4c each cost about
   that, including the two negatives.

Prerequisite either way: **provenance** — which committed parts of `q` a
refutation actually depended on. That is lazy-clause-generation's
explanation discipline (propagators must explain their prunings), it is
what the mkcdcl prototype choked on (disequality provenance blow-up, per
Michael; plus prune-only — no propagation — and the divergence gap the
termination view has since closed), and the first-order representation
is its natural substrate. The first-order rep item thereby gains a third
motivation, but its design does not change.

## Gap 3: deduction at stalls (lookahead/SAC), not memory

When a conde/d stalls with k>1 live clauses, unit propagation learns
nothing — yet the *intersection* of the surviving clauses' store
extensions is sound to commit (failed-literal probing / singleton arc
consistency / the Extended Andorra Model's branch-and-merge). Would it
fire here? Structural prediction: rarely, for the interpreter and type
views — surviving clauses of eval-expo/d disagree by construction
(number vs cons vs match produce disjoint extensions on the candidate),
and type-ofo/d's clauses partition by type. Where it classically fires
is FD-style domains (all surviving values of a cell agree it's in
{2,3}) — but there, the right upgrade is real domain constraints (CP
propagators), not per-stall guard re-evaluation at k× guard cost under
ce1. Leave unbuilt; if the FD direction (backlog: Sudoku, crossover
mapping) returns, revisit as domain propagation rather than lookahead.

## Verdict

**Not now, and mostly not CDCL.** The three gaps decompose into: a
2.7×-capped ID overhead owned by explicit search (already backlog Now);
a lemma-learning opportunity that hand-written views have saturated on
the current suite, with good cross-task transfer so far; and a
low-predicted-yield lookahead whose natural home (FD) wants domain
propagators instead. The information-poverty diagnosis stands: the
binding constraint on this project is *sources of information* (new
views, richer specs — see the info-sources survey note), not the
strength of the reasoning principle applied to the sources we have.

**Trigger conditions to revisit** (make this falsifiable):

1. Benchmark broadening shows per-task refutation families the fixed
   stack misses — i.e. the sample→diagnose→refute loop stops converging,
   or its per-task human cost starts dominating. Designed measurement,
   cheap to build when needed: a refuted-candidate logging hook (dual of
   `*sample-term-every*`, firing on follower refutations, tagged by
   which view refuted), then cluster refuted terms syntactically. Few
   large task-specific clusters ⇒ in-search learning pays.
2. The explicit scheduler lands and its profile shows heavy same-reason
   refutation across non-prefix-shared contexts even with the full view
   stack — the first-order rep will make that visible (and provides the
   provenance substrate a learner would need).

Related work anchors: Neo (PLDI 2018) conflict-driven synthesis; Popper
(MLJ 2021) learning-from-failures as ASP constraints; lazy clause
generation (Ohrimenko/Stuckey/Codish) for the propagators-must-explain
discipline; Extended Andorra Model for stall-time merging.
